## Draft

If you own rental property, the tax code just shifted in your favor.

The "One Big Beautiful Bill" passed last July, and most landlords haven't caught up with what it means for their 2025 returns. Here are three changes that matter.

### 1. 100% Bonus Depreciation Is Back

For the past few years, bonus depreciation was phasing out. That's over.

Starting January 2025, you can deduct 100% of the cost of short-lived assets in the year you buy them. We're talking appliances, carpets, fixtures—anything with a shorter useful life than the building itself.

**What this means for you**: That $3,000 HVAC unit you installed last summer? Deduct it all this year instead of spreading it over multiple years.

The building itself still depreciates over 27.5 years. But everything inside it? Immediate write-off.

### 2. No SALT Cap on Investment Properties

You've probably heard about the $10,000 cap on state and local tax deductions. Here's what most people miss:

**That cap only applies to your personal residence.**

Your rental properties? You can deduct 100% of the property taxes through Schedule E. No cap. No limit.

If you're paying $8,000 in property taxes on your primary home and $15,000 across your rentals, only the $8,000 is subject to the cap. The $15,000 flows through as a business expense.

### 3. The $25,000 Offset Most People Miss

If you actively participate in managing your rentals and your modified adjusted gross income is under $100,000 (married filing jointly), you can use up to $25,000 of rental losses to offset your W-2 income.

Read that again.

This means if your rental shows a loss on paper—after depreciation, repairs, and other deductions—you can use that loss to reduce the taxes on your day job income.

The full $25,000 is available if your MAGI is ≤ $100,000. It phases out between $100,000 and $150,000.

---

### What To Do Now

1. **Review your asset purchases from 2025** - Anything that qualifies for bonus depreciation should be documented and ready for your preparer.

2. **Separate your property taxes** - Make sure your tax professional knows which properties are rentals vs. personal residence.

3. **Calculate your MAGI** - If you're near the $100,000 threshold, there may be strategies to stay under and claim the full $25,000 offset.

The tax code isn't getting simpler. But for landlords, it just got more favorable.
